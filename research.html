Research

My research asks how and why people navigate and interact with their daily environments in different ways. It has three connected lines: modeling personality dynamics, measuring them, and using machine learning to represent and predict them. Across all three, I aim for models that describe each individual while still supporting conclusions about people in general.

  • Person and situation dynamics
  • Psychological measurement
  • AI and LLMs

Person and situation dynamics

Lewin's formula, B = f(P, E), says behavior depends on the person and the environment but leaves the form of that function open. I build Bayesian models that specify it at increasing levels of individualization: how a person's range of behavior depends on the situation, how behavior and situations influence each other over time, and the personal structure behind both.

Psychological measurement

Studying daily life means measuring people many times, so measures must be brief while staying reliable and valid across people, contexts, and kinds of data. I develop adaptive assessments, models of how items function in different situations, and measures beyond self report, such as smartphone cognitive tests and peer reports.

AI and LLMs

Broad traits such as the Big Five are a useful summary, but they compress much of what makes people different. I use machine learning and language models to represent people, situations, and life events, and I test whether AI based measures keep the same meaning across groups.
